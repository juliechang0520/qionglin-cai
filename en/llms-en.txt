# The Cai Clan of Qionglin, Kinmen (English summary)

A guide to the Cai (Tsai / Chua) clan of Qionglin village in Kinmen, Taiwan: the "seven buildings, eight halls" national monument, Ming and Qing scholar-officials, the spring and autumn ancestral rites, and how to visit.

## History
- Early 12th c. (Southern Song): Cai Shiqilang (蔡十七郎) marries into the Chen family of Pinglin and founds the Qionglin Cai line. The Chens later move away and Pinglin becomes a single-surname Cai village.
- Song–early Ming: The fifth-generation ancestor Jingshan (靜山公) is the common ancestor of all living members. His descendants divide into two lines and nine branches (九房), living in four neighbourhoods (四甲).
- 1519: Cai Sen becomes the first member to hold office. The family begins its tradition of "farming and study."
- 1529: Cai Zongdao compiles the first genealogy from a worm-eaten family chart. It was revised six times, last in 1823.
- 1568–1634: Five members pass the highest imperial examination (jinshi) under the Ming.
- Late Ming: The village is renamed "Qionglin" by imperial grant, linked to the scholar Cai Xianchen. Sources differ on whether this was in the Tianqi or Chongzhen reign.
- 1663: Qing forces order the coast evacuated. More than half the clan dies or flees; one member carries the genealogy to safety.
- 1770: The Grand Ancestral Temple is rebuilt in the form seen today.
- 1845: Cai Tinglan, born in Penghu to a Qionglin family, passes the jinshi examination.
- 1937–1992: Japanese troops stable horses in the temple; after 1949 the halls serve as classrooms, depots and a field hospital. A 1,355-metre combat tunnel is completed in 1976.
- 1985: The seven buildings are designated a historic monument (today a national monument).
- 2011: The clan's ancestral rite is registered as intangible cultural heritage of Kinmen County.

## Seven buildings, eight halls
- Cai Family Ancestral Temple (Grand Ancestral Hall) (蔡氏家廟（大宗）; 1st generation · whole clan; Rebuilt 1770): Honours the founding ancestor and the first five generations, plus clan members who held office. The spring and autumn rites are held here.
- Zhuxi Ancestral Hall (六世竹溪公宗祠; 6th generation; Late Ming): Shared by four branches. Its lantern title, "Provincial Military Commander," comes from the general Cai Panlong.
- Lepu Ancestral Hall (六世樂圃公宗祠; 6th generation; Late Ming): Holds the most plaques of any hall, including the imperial plaque that renamed the village "Qionglin."
- Tingfu Ancestral Hall (十世廷輔公宗祠; 10th generation; Late Ming): Occupies the third hall of the Lepu building, with its own side entrance: two halls under one roof, which is why there are eight halls in seven buildings.
- Qianting Branch Hall (六世前庭房宗祠; 6th generation; Shortly after 1770): Said to have been built from materials left over from the 1770 rebuilding. No member of this branch held office, so it is the only hall not allowed barrel roof tiles, a mark of rank.
- Boya Ancestral Hall (十世柏崖公宗祠; 10th generation; Kangxi–Qianlong era): Hall of the Dacuo branch, the earliest branch to produce an official (1519).
- 11th-Generation Ancestral Hall (十一世宗祠; 11th generation; 1840): Paid for by the merchant Cai Weiting and his son on the site of a Ming official residence. Its rear hall honours ancestors adopted into the clan from other families.
- Fanbo Shrine (十六世藩伯宗祠; 16th generation; Qianlong era; rebuilt 1932): A shrine for one man, Cai Shouyu, provincial administration commissioner of Yunnan. Its red gate is said to be an imperial honour.

## People
- Cai Guiyi 蔡貴易 (jinshi 1568): Rose to provincial vice-commissioner. With his son Cai Xianchen he is remembered as "father and son, both masters of letters" (父子文宗).
- Cai Shouyu 蔡守愚 (jinshi 1586): Placed 13th in the second rank; became administration commissioner of Yunnan. His father died fighting pirates. The Fanbo Shrine is his.
- Cai Xianchen 蔡獻臣 (jinshi 1589): Ranked 6th in the second rank; a respected scholar who corresponded with leading writers and argued for Kinmen's coastal defence. The village name is associated with him.
- Cai Maoxian 蔡懋賢 (jinshi 1589): A nephew adopted into the family. Despite his degree, his ancestral tablet could not enter the Grand Ancestral Temple, a sign of how strictly the clan kept to descent by blood.
- Cai Guoguang 蔡國光 (jinshi 1634): One of the last Ming jinshi; later wrote the preface to the genealogy rebuilt after the 1663 evacuation.
- Cai Tinglan 蔡廷蘭 (jinshi 1845): Born in Penghu. After a typhoon carried him to Vietnam he wrote an account of the journey, later translated into several languages; an English translation appeared in 2023.
- Cai Panlong 蔡攀龍 (general, 1738–1798): A poor fisherman who joined the army, helped put down the Lin Shuangwen uprising in Taiwan (1787), rose to provincial military commander, and had his portrait hung in the Hall of Purple Light in Beijing.

## The spring and autumn rites
- When: The spring rite falls on the 7th day of the 2nd lunar month and the autumn rite on the 6th day of the 10th lunar month, the death anniversaries of the fifth-generation ancestor and his wife. (One older source gives the 6th and 7th instead.) Dates in the Western calendar change every year.
- Two rounds: Ancestral tablets are brought out twice. The first round honours the founding ancestor and the earliest generations; the second honours the fifth-generation ancestor Jingshan and his wife, with sixth-generation ancestors and office-holders beside them. Each round is a full "great three offerings" (大三獻) ceremony.
- Who runs it: Twenty hosts (頭家) share the work: eighteen from Qionglin and one each from the villages of Longkou and Qianshuitou. Ten days before the rite they draw lots to divide tasks such as steamed red turtle cakes, offering dishes and hiring musicians. Men newly married serve as hosts at their second rite.
- The feast: After the rite the clan shares a meal of fixed, simple dishes, a reminder of the ancestors' frugality.
- Other rites: The day before Qingming the clan visits the fifth-generation ancestor's tomb at the foot of Mount Taiwu. On New Year's Eve the second-generation ancestress's tomb receives an offering that must include pig's liver, as she asked in her will.
- Visitors: The rite is a family ceremony. In autumn 2026 it was opened to a small number of non-clan observers for the first time; ask locally before attending.

## Visiting
- Where: Qionglin village, Jinhu Township, Kinmen County, Taiwan, in the centre of Kinmen island. Bus No. 2 stops at "Qionglin" (瓊林) by the village office.
- The halls: The ancestral halls are open to visitors and are among the few in Kinmen that welcome the public.
- Qionglin Combat Tunnel: 1,355 metres long with 12 entrances, open for walking. Entrance near the village office.
- Wind lion gods: Stone guardians at the north-east (193 cm) and south-west (114 cm) edges of the village, both county monuments. A small smiling one sits on the back wall of the Grand Ancestral Temple.
- Guided walks: Kinmen's tourism office lists a two-hour Qionglin walking tour on weekend afternoons (daily in summer). Check the official tourism site for current times.

## FAQ
- Q: What does "seven buildings, eight halls" mean?
  A: Qionglin has eight ancestral halls in seven buildings. The Lepu and Tingfu halls share one three-hall building, with the Tingfu hall in the rear and its own side entrance.
- Q: Who are the Cai of Qionglin?
  A: A clan descended from Cai Shiqilang, who settled in Pinglin (later Qionglin) in the 12th century. Members spell the surname Cai, Tsai or (in Hokkien-speaking Southeast Asia) Chua. Branches emigrated to Penghu, Taiwan, Xiamen and Southeast Asia.
- Q: When are the ancestral rites held?
  A: On the 7th day of the 2nd lunar month and the 6th day of the 10th lunar month each year, in the Grand Ancestral Temple.
- Q: Can tourists visit?
  A: Yes. The halls are open to visitors, and the village also has a walkable combat tunnel and two wind lion god statues. Please be respectful during rites.
- Q: Why is the village so well preserved?
  A: Decades of military rule limited development, Kinmen escaped the destruction of temples in mainland China, and after 1992 the national park and the clan restored the buildings.
