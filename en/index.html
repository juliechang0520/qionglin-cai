KINMEN · TAIWAN

金門瓊林蔡氏The Cai Clan of Qionglin

For nearly nine hundred years one family has lived at the centre of Kinmen island. Qionglin (瓊林; also spelled Chiung-lin, Hokkien Khêng-lîm) is a single-surname village of the Cai clan (蔡; also Tsai or Chua), known for its eight ancestral halls, its Ming dynasty scholar-officials and an ancestral rite still performed in the old way.

HISTORY

From Pinglin to Qionglin

  • Early 12th c. (Southern Song)Cai Shiqilang (蔡十七郎) marries into the Chen family of Pinglin and founds the Qionglin Cai line. The Chens later move away and Pinglin becomes a single-surname Cai village.
  • Song–early MingThe fifth-generation ancestor Jingshan (靜山公) is the common ancestor of all living members. His descendants divide into two lines and nine branches (九房), living in four neighbourhoods (四甲).
  • 1519Cai Sen becomes the first member to hold office. The family begins its tradition of "farming and study."
  • 1529Cai Zongdao compiles the first genealogy from a worm-eaten family chart. It was revised six times, last in 1823.
  • 1568–1634Five members pass the highest imperial examination (jinshi) under the Ming.
  • Late MingThe village is renamed "Qionglin" by imperial grant, linked to the scholar Cai Xianchen. Sources differ on whether this was in the Tianqi or Chongzhen reign.
  • 1663Qing forces order the coast evacuated. More than half the clan dies or flees; one member carries the genealogy to safety.
  • 1770The Grand Ancestral Temple is rebuilt in the form seen today.
  • 1845Cai Tinglan, born in Penghu to a Qionglin family, passes the jinshi examination.
  • 1937–1992Japanese troops stable horses in the temple; after 1949 the halls serve as classrooms, depots and a field hospital. A 1,355-metre combat tunnel is completed in 1976.
  • 1985The seven buildings are designated a historic monument (today a national monument).
  • 2011The clan's ancestral rite is registered as intangible cultural heritage of Kinmen County.
ARCHITECTURE

Seven buildings, eight halls

Each hall belongs to a different level of the family tree, from the whole clan down to a single honoured ancestor. Together they were designated a historic monument in 1985.

Cai Family Ancestral Temple (Grand Ancestral Hall)

蔡氏家廟（大宗）
1st generation · whole clan · Rebuilt 1770

Honours the founding ancestor and the first five generations, plus clan members who held office. The spring and autumn rites are held here.

Zhuxi Ancestral Hall

六世竹溪公宗祠
6th generation · Late Ming

Shared by four branches. Its lantern title, "Provincial Military Commander," comes from the general Cai Panlong.

Lepu Ancestral Hall

六世樂圃公宗祠
6th generation · Late Ming

Holds the most plaques of any hall, including the imperial plaque that renamed the village "Qionglin."

Tingfu Ancestral Hall

十世廷輔公宗祠
10th generation · Late Ming

Occupies the third hall of the Lepu building, with its own side entrance: two halls under one roof, which is why there are eight halls in seven buildings.

Qianting Branch Hall

六世前庭房宗祠
6th generation · Shortly after 1770

Said to have been built from materials left over from the 1770 rebuilding. No member of this branch held office, so it is the only hall not allowed barrel roof tiles, a mark of rank.

Boya Ancestral Hall

十世柏崖公宗祠
10th generation · Kangxi–Qianlong era

Hall of the Dacuo branch, the earliest branch to produce an official (1519).

11th-Generation Ancestral Hall

十一世宗祠
11th generation · 1840

Paid for by the merchant Cai Weiting and his son on the site of a Ming official residence. Its rear hall honours ancestors adopted into the clan from other families.

Fanbo Shrine

十六世藩伯宗祠
16th generation · Qianlong era; rebuilt 1932

A shrine for one man, Cai Shouyu, provincial administration commissioner of Yunnan. Its red gate is said to be an imperial honour.

Walking into an ancestral temple

A hall is a path from the public street to the most sacred space at the back.

1. Forecourt 前埕

Where the clan gathers on rite days; stone flagpole bases mark degrees won.

2. Entrance hall 三川門

The formal front with three doors; the centre door opens only for major ceremonies.

3. Courtyard 天井

An open-air court for light, air and drainage. Rain flows inward, so good fortune stays in the house.

4. Side galleries 兩廊

Covered walkways where offerings wait and attendants stand.

5. Main hall 正廳

The most important space, hung with examination plaques and family mottos.

6. Ancestral altar 祖龕

At the very back, tablets are arranged by generation, with the founder at the centre.

PEOPLE

Scholars and a general

The clan's lantern reads 文武世家, "a family of letters and arms": Ming scholars, then a Qing general. In all, the clan produced six jinshi, seven provincial graduates, fifteen tribute students and six military officers.

  • Cai Guiyi 蔡貴易
    jinshi 1568    Rose to provincial vice-commissioner. With his son Cai Xianchen he is remembered as "father and son, both masters of letters" (父子文宗).
  • Cai Shouyu 蔡守愚
    jinshi 1586    Placed 13th in the second rank; became administration commissioner of Yunnan. His father died fighting pirates. The Fanbo Shrine is his.
  • Cai Xianchen 蔡獻臣
    jinshi 1589    Ranked 6th in the second rank; a respected scholar who corresponded with leading writers and argued for Kinmen's coastal defence. The village name is associated with him.
  • Cai Maoxian 蔡懋賢
    jinshi 1589    A nephew adopted into the family. Despite his degree, his ancestral tablet could not enter the Grand Ancestral Temple, a sign of how strictly the clan kept to descent by blood.
  • Cai Guoguang 蔡國光
    jinshi 1634    One of the last Ming jinshi; later wrote the preface to the genealogy rebuilt after the 1663 evacuation.
  • Cai Tinglan 蔡廷蘭
    jinshi 1845    Born in Penghu. After a typhoon carried him to Vietnam he wrote an account of the journey, later translated into several languages; an English translation appeared in 2023.
  • Cai Panlong 蔡攀龍
    general, 1738–1798    A poor fisherman who joined the army, helped put down the Lin Shuangwen uprising in Taiwan (1787), rose to provincial military commander, and had his portrait hung in the Hall of Purple Light in Beijing.
RITES

The spring and autumn rites

The Qionglin Cai ancestral rite was registered as intangible cultural heritage of Kinmen County in 2011. Today a clan foundation covers most of the cost.

  • WhenThe spring rite falls on the 7th day of the 2nd lunar month and the autumn rite on the 6th day of the 10th lunar month, the death anniversaries of the fifth-generation ancestor and his wife. (One older source gives the 6th and 7th instead.) Dates in the Western calendar change every year.
  • Two roundsAncestral tablets are brought out twice. The first round honours the founding ancestor and the earliest generations; the second honours the fifth-generation ancestor Jingshan and his wife, with sixth-generation ancestors and office-holders beside them. Each round is a full "great three offerings" (大三獻) ceremony.
  • Who runs itTwenty hosts (頭家) share the work: eighteen from Qionglin and one each from the villages of Longkou and Qianshuitou. Ten days before the rite they draw lots to divide tasks such as steamed red turtle cakes, offering dishes and hiring musicians. Men newly married serve as hosts at their second rite.
  • The feastAfter the rite the clan shares a meal of fixed, simple dishes, a reminder of the ancestors' frugality.
  • Other ritesThe day before Qingming the clan visits the fifth-generation ancestor's tomb at the foot of Mount Taiwu. On New Year's Eve the second-generation ancestress's tomb receives an offering that must include pig's liver, as she asked in her will.
  • VisitorsThe rite is a family ceremony. In autumn 2026 it was opened to a small number of non-clan observers for the first time; ask locally before attending.
VISIT

Visiting Qionglin

  • WhereQionglin village, Jinhu Township, Kinmen County, Taiwan, in the centre of Kinmen island. Bus No. 2 stops at "Qionglin" (瓊林) by the village office.
  • The hallsThe ancestral halls are open to visitors and are among the few in Kinmen that welcome the public.
  • Qionglin Combat Tunnel1,355 metres long with 12 entrances, open for walking. Entrance near the village office.
  • Wind lion godsStone guardians at the north-east (193 cm) and south-west (114 cm) edges of the village, both county monuments. A small smiling one sits on the back wall of the Grand Ancestral Temple.
  • Guided walksKinmen's tourism office lists a two-hour Qionglin walking tour on weekend afternoons (daily in summer). Check the official tourism site for current times.

FAQ

Questions

What does "seven buildings, eight halls" mean?

Qionglin has eight ancestral halls in seven buildings. The Lepu and Tingfu halls share one three-hall building, with the Tingfu hall in the rear and its own side entrance.

Who are the Cai of Qionglin?

A clan descended from Cai Shiqilang, who settled in Pinglin (later Qionglin) in the 12th century. Members spell the surname Cai, Tsai or (in Hokkien-speaking Southeast Asia) Chua. Branches emigrated to Penghu, Taiwan, Xiamen and Southeast Asia.

When are the ancestral rites held?

On the 7th day of the 2nd lunar month and the 6th day of the 10th lunar month each year, in the Grand Ancestral Temple.

Can tourists visit?

Yes. The halls are open to visitors, and the village also has a walkable combat tunnel and two wind lion god statues. Please be respectful during rites.

Why is the village so well preserved?

Decades of military rule limited development, Kinmen escaped the destruction of temples in mainland China, and after 1992 the national park and the clan restored the buildings.
